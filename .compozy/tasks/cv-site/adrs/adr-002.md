# ADR-002: No contact form or contact details in v1 — outbound links only

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-09

## Context

A CV site for job hunting normally invites inbound contact. The owner, however,
wants to avoid spam and harvesting of personal contact details. The owner
already maintains established professional profiles (GitHub, LinkedIn, and
other sites) that provide their own vetted contact channels.

## Decision

v1 includes no contact form and no raw contact details (no email address, no
phone number) rendered on the page. Instead, the site presents an outbound
links section that routes visitors to the owner's existing professional
profiles, where contact happens through those platforms' own controls.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Contact form with spam protection

- **Description**: A form with anti-spam measures (e.g., challenge, rate limit).
- **Pros**: Captures inbound interest directly on the site.
- **Cons**: Adds maintenance and a spam surface the owner explicitly wants to
  avoid; collects messages the owner did not ask to manage in v1.
- **Why rejected**: Conflicts with the stated goal of avoiding spam and inbound
  message handling.

### Alternative 2: Visible email address

- **Description**: Display a clickable email address.
- **Pros**: Zero-friction contact path.
- **Cons**: Email is easily scraped and leads to spam.
- **Why rejected**: Directly contradicts the owner's anti-spam requirement.

## Consequences

### Positive

- No spam surface and no exposed personal contact details.
- Contact is funneled through platforms the owner already controls.

### Negative

- No direct, on-site way for a recruiter to message the owner; they must use an
  external profile.

### Risks

- Risk: a recruiter unwilling to leave the page drops off. Mitigation: make the
  links section prominent and unambiguous so the next step is obvious.

## Implementation Notes

If a downloadable PDF resume is included, confirm whether it should carry direct
contact details given the same anti-spam concern. Captured as an open question
in the PRD.
