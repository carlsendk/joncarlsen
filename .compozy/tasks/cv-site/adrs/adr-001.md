# ADR-001: Single-page scrolling CV as the v1 product shape

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-09

## Context

The owner is building a personal CV site whose primary job is job hunting in a
leadership/management capacity. Recruiters typically scan a candidate site for
under 30 seconds, mobile traffic dominates (~75%+), and load time directly
affects whether the page is read at all. The site must establish credibility
fast and route visitors to existing professional profiles. The owner explicitly
does not want a contact form or contact details on the site in v1 to avoid spam,
so the site's job is to present and link out, not to collect inbound messages.

## Decision

Build v1 as a single-page, vertically scrolling CV with four stacked sections:
Hero + summary, Experience timeline, Impact highlights, and an outbound links
footer (GitHub, LinkedIn, other profiles, optional PDF resume). One page, one
scroll, mobile-first, no contact form.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Multi-page portfolio

- **Description**: Separate pages for Home, Experience, deep Impact case
  studies, and Links.
- **Pros**: More room for leadership storytelling; deeper case studies.
- **Cons**: More navigation friction; more pages to maintain; recruiters
  scanning quickly may never reach inner pages.
- **Why rejected**: The <30s recruiter scan favors a single scannable surface;
  the maintenance and navigation cost is not justified for a job-hunting v1.

### Alternative 2: Resume-first minimal

- **Description**: A polished one-page mirror of the PDF resume that links out.
- **Pros**: Fastest to ship; near-zero maintenance.
- **Cons**: Least differentiation; reads as a static document rather than a
  professional presence; weak for a leadership brand.
- **Why rejected**: Too undifferentiated to strengthen a leadership job hunt;
  the single-page scrolling approach achieves similar low maintenance while
  presenting impact more compellingly.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Fast, mobile-first, scannable in a single scroll — matches recruiter behavior.
- Low maintenance; a single content surface to keep current.
- Clear, frictionless path out to existing profiles.

### Negative

- Limited depth for individual impact stories compared to dedicated case-study
  pages.
- All content competes for attention on one page; ordering and brevity matter.

### Risks

- Risk: impact stories feel shallow. Mitigation: lead each highlight with a
  quantified outcome and keep supporting detail tight.
- Risk: single page grows long and unscannable. Mitigation: cap section counts
  and enforce above-the-fold clarity in the hero.

## Implementation Notes

Framework and hosting are deferred to the TechSpec. The page must be mobile-first
and load fast. No contact form or raw contact details are included in v1.
