# ADR-002: Always-visible, pure-CSS sticky behavior (no scroll-driven JavaScript)

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-10

## Context

A sticky header can behave in several ways as the reader scrolls: stay pinned at
all times, hide on scroll-down and reveal on scroll-up, or appear only after the
reader passes the first screen. The latter two are popular in 2026 but require a
JavaScript scroll listener and introduce accessibility hazards — a header that
moves can obscure a keyboard-focused element and break skip-link behavior.

This site is deliberately static with near-zero JavaScript (the only script is
the theme toggle), uses semantic design tokens, and ships a print stylesheet.
The owner confirmed the header should stay **always visible**.

## Decision

The header is **always visible**, pinned via CSS `position: sticky; top: 0`
with no scroll-driven JavaScript. It never hides, shrinks, or animates on scroll.
The only script associated with the header remains the existing theme-toggle
logic. A subtle separation (the translucent blurred surface with a hairline
bottom border from the PRD) distinguishes it from content beneath without motion.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Hide on scroll-down, reveal on scroll-up

- **Description**: The header slides out when scrolling down and returns when
  scrolling up.
- **Pros**: Reclaims vertical space on small viewports; a common modern pattern.
- **Cons**: Needs a JS scroll listener; can hide keyboard focus and disrupt
  skip-links; adds motion to a deliberately quiet design.
- **Why rejected**: Conflicts with the near-zero-JS ethos and adds avoidable
  accessibility risk for a single-column reading experience.

### Alternative 2: Appear only after the fold

- **Description**: Header is absent at the top and fades in once the reader
  scrolls past the first screen.
- **Pros**: Cleaner first paint on home where the `Hero` already shows identity.
- **Cons**: Requires JS to detect scroll position; the header's presence becomes
  inconsistent; reintroduces motion and focus concerns.
- **Why rejected**: The owner chose always-visible; consistency and simplicity
  win over a marginally cleaner first paint.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Zero added JavaScript; no scroll listeners to maintain or debounce.
- No focus-obscuring movement; predictable, accessible, print-safe behavior.
- The header is reachable at any scroll position on every page.

### Negative

- The bar permanently consumes a strip of vertical space (mitigated by keeping it
  compact — well under ~10% of viewport height).

### Risks

- On very short viewports the fixed strip could crowd content. Mitigation: keep
  the header height small and the content layout unchanged beneath it.

## Implementation Notes

- Pure CSS `position: sticky` avoids the body top-padding workarounds that fixed
  positioning requires.
- The header must be hidden in print output alongside the other chrome.
