# ADR-002: Self-hosted subset webfonts (Fraunces + JetBrains Mono), system-sans body

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-09

## Context

The editorial direction (ADR-001) needs a display face and a monospace face. The
site has a no-tracking stance (cv-site ADR-002) and tight Lighthouse budgets
(perf >= 90). Web-font load cost is the PRD's top-listed risk. Two delivery models
exist: a font CDN (e.g. Google Fonts) or self-hosting. A CDN issues a third-party
request that exposes every visitor's IP to the font host — incompatible with the
no-tracking posture — and adds a cross-origin connection on the critical path.

## Decision

Self-host **Fraunces** (display serif, headlines/hero) and **JetBrains Mono**
(dates, labels, metadata), subset to the Latin range and served as `woff2` from
`public/fonts/`. Declare them with `@font-face` (`font-display: swap`) and
`<link rel="preload">` the two files actually used above the fold. **Body text
uses a system-sans stack** (`-apple-system, Segoe UI, Roboto, …`) — zero network
cost — so only two webfonts load. Expose the faces as Tailwind v4 `@theme` tokens
(`--font-display`, `--font-mono`, `--font-sans`).

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Google Fonts (or other) CDN

- **Pros**: zero hosting, automatic subsetting, cached across sites.
- **Cons**: leaks visitor IPs to a third party (violates the no-tracking stance);
  extra cross-origin connection on the critical path.
- **Why rejected**: the privacy violation alone disqualifies it; self-hosting is
  also the faster, more controllable option.

### Alternative 2: System fonts only (no webfonts)

- **Pros**: zero load cost, simplest.
- **Cons**: cannot deliver the distinctive editorial character the PRD requires;
  every CV looks the same.
- **Why rejected**: fails the core "premium, distinctive, editorial" goal.

### Alternative 3: A different pairing (Space Grotesk / Newsreader + IBM Plex Mono)

- **Why rejected**: the owner selected Fraunces + JetBrains Mono as the most
  distinctive premium-editorial fit; the others were offered and not chosen.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Distinctive editorial type with no third-party tracking and full version control.
- Only two webfonts on the wire; body text paints instantly on the system stack.

### Negative

- Must subset and commit ~2-4 `woff2` files to the repo and maintain them.
- A FOUT window exists (`swap`); mitigated by preload + the system-sans body.

### Risks

- Unbudgeted font weight could regress perf — keep to the weights actually used,
  subset to Latin, and re-verify Lighthouse after adding fonts.

## Amendment (2026-06-10): self-host the body face too

**What changed.** Body text now uses self-hosted **Hanken Grotesk** (latin-subset
variable woff2, font-display: swap, preloaded) instead of the system-sans stack.
The system stack is retained only as the --font-sans fallback during the swap
window. Three faces now load: Fraunces (display), JetBrains Mono (labels/meta),
Hanken Grotesk (body).

**Why.** The system stack was the one un-crafted font on the page and the largest
by usage (all body prose), so it varied per device and read as inconsistent next
to the two self-hosted editorial faces. A single self-hosted body face makes type
identical across machines and pairs cleanly with Fraunces. Hanken Grotesk was
chosen over Inter/Roboto (too generic) and IBM Plex / Mona Sans as the warmest
humanist fit.

**Cost accepted.** One additional latin-subset woff2 (~35 KB), preloaded. This
consciously spends part of the web-font budget the original decision protected;
re-verify the Lighthouse perf budget (>= 90). The no-tracking posture is
unaffected (still self-hosted, same-origin).
