# ADR-002: Two-tier project model (featured deep case studies plus a projects index)

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-10

## Context

The owner has roughly twenty projects available (from `docs/projekts.html` and
his career history), of which three have strong existing narrative, a handful are
developable, and about eleven are title-only today. Best practice for a
leadership portfolio is to feature a few high-impact projects deeply and keep the
rest compact, so a reviewer can scan in under two minutes. Putting all twenty on
the CV would bloat it and dilute the strongest work.

## Decision

Organise projects in two tiers from the same tagged source:

- **Featured projects**: a small set (a handful) rendered as full, deep case-study
  pages using the standard template (Context, what I did, Outcome, with a metrics
  strip). These are the high-impact stories.
- **Projects index**: a dedicated page listing every project, grouped by company,
  each as a short entry (title, one line, company, period, optional metric).
  Featured entries link to their deep page; the rest are compact list items.

The frontpage and the full CV show only the top featured projects plus a clear
link to the projects index. New projects are added to the index over time; a
project is promoted to a featured case study when its narrative is written.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Every project as a full case study

- **Description**: Twenty full case-study pages and cards.
- **Pros**: Uniform treatment.
- **Cons**: Heavy to write and read; dilutes the high-impact few; about eleven
  projects have no narrative yet, so most pages would be thin.
- **Why rejected**: Contradicts "few high-impact beat listing everything" and
  creates a wall of thin pages.

### Alternative 2: Inline compact list on the CV (no index page)

- **Description**: Featured case studies plus a long "more projects" list on the
  full CV.
- **Pros**: No new route.
- **Cons**: A twenty-item list makes the CV long and harder to scan.
- **Why rejected**: A dedicated index keeps the CV short while still exposing the
  full set.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The CV stays scannable; the strongest projects get depth; the full breadth is
  still one click away.
- Adding a project is a small index entry; promoting one to featured is writing
  its narrative. Growth is incremental.

### Negative

- One more route and listing surface to maintain.
- Two presentations of a project (index entry and, if featured, a deep page) must
  stay consistent. Mitigation: both derive from the one tagged source.

### Risks

- The index could fill with title-only stubs that read as unfinished. Mitigation:
  index entries require at least a one-line description; bare titles are not
  published.

## Implementation Notes

Routing for the projects index, the featured flag, and grouping are deferred to
the TechSpec. The existing `work` case-study collection and design system are
reused and extended.
